# Schwartz awareness: the entry-point layer

Assume the book. The five stages are not re-taught here. What follows is the
diagnostic, where to record your own awareness map, and the practical rules
that override Schwartz on modern surfaces.

## What this skill owns

`copy-strategist` calls it as step 1. It settles three things and then hands
off: the entry point where the copy starts talking, the message angle, and the
length. It locks before desire, because a reader in the wrong stage cannot be
reached by any driver.

## The five stages, compressed

Unaware means no felt problem. Problem-aware means the pain is felt and the
solution is unknown. Solution-aware means the category is known and yours is
not. Product-aware means yours is known and unchosen. Most-aware means the only
open question is terms.

The move that matters: enter at the reader's stage and walk forward one step at
a time. Copy fails when it opens at a stage ahead of the reader.

## Diagnosing the stage

Traffic source is the first read. Brand search and retargeting run late,
product and problem search run through the middle, and a social scroll or a
cold email runs early because nobody asked to hear from you.

Behaviour is the second read and it overrides the first, because someone who
arrived cold and then read three comparison posts is not cold any more. Pricing
pages and repeated opens indicate product-aware. A single blog read indicates
solution-aware at best.

The fast version, when neither signal settles it: imagine saying the product
name to this person. "What's that" puts them early, "is that the thing that
solves X" puts them at solution-aware, "that's like [competitor]" puts them at
product-aware, and "I've been meaning to buy that" puts them at most-aware.

When source and behaviour disagree and the fast version does not settle it,
ask. This is one of the three questions `copy-strategist` may ask before
drafting.

## Your awareness map

If you have one, point this skill at it. A useful map answers three questions:

1. **Which channel serves which stage.** For example: social is rented reach
   serving the early stages, the site and newsletter are the owned middle
   carrying solution-aware to product-aware, and the sales page or booking
   page carries product-aware to most-aware.
2. **How vocabulary deepens with awareness.** Plain-language pain at the top,
   the category and your thesis in the middle, shown work and mechanics at the
   bottom.
3. **Which stages are real for your audience.** Some audiences have no genuine
   unaware segment (see below). Write that down so drafts stop explaining what
   the reader already knows.

With no map, diagnose per brief from the signals above.

## Rules that override Schwartz in practice

**Find your practical floor.** Some audiences cannot be unaware of the topic.
Nobody in a marketing audience is unaware that AI exists, for example, so
opening by explaining it wastes the most valuable line. When that is true,
treat problem-aware as the floor, and read "unaware" as unaware of the
specific gap you name rather than of the whole topic.

**Format caps length before Schwartz gets a say.** Schwartz is right that
colder readers need more words, which holds for a landing page and breaks on a
feed. A mobile social post shows roughly 140 characters above the fold whatever
the stage, and a short-form video still runs 30 to 90 seconds. Stage decides
how much explaining the body does, and it buys no extra room above the fold.

**Bias short.** Over-explaining is the most common failure in drafted copy.
When the stage argues for a longer piece, draft the tighter version and offer
the longer one as the option.

## For work on other businesses

A client or company deep dive uses Schwartz unmodified. Your own audience
narrowing does not transfer, and the subject's readers may genuinely be
unaware.

## Handoff

Return the diagnosed stage with the evidence behind it, the entry point, and
the length call, so `cashvertising` picks its driver against a settled stage.
Say explicitly when the stage was inferred rather than confirmed.

If you have a voice guide or copy checker, anything drafted here, including
suggested openers, goes through it before it is handed back.

## Boundaries

It does not pick the desire, own the structure, or set the offer. It cannot
tell you what the reader actually knows, only what the signals imply, which is
why the ask exists.
