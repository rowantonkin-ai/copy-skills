# Ca$hvertising: the Desire layer

Assume the book. Whitman's argument, that eight desires are wired in and good
copy channels them rather than manufacturing them, is not reproduced here.
What follows is the routing vocabulary, the moves that survive a modern,
sceptical reader, and the ones that do not.

## What this skill owns

`copy-strategist` calls it as step 2 of the five-step stack: lock the ONE
Life-Force 8 driver, build the means-end chain, then supply belief triggers
back at step 4. It is also useful on its own for picking the driver of a
single hook.

It picks the desire and engineers belief. Awareness belongs to
`copywriting-awareness`, structure to `boron-letters`, offer and CTA to
`dan-kennedy-copy`.

## The taxonomy

**Life-Force 8.** Survival and life extension (1), enjoyment of food and drink
(2), freedom from fear, pain and danger (3), sexual companionship (4),
comfortable living conditions (5), superiority and winning (6), care of loved
ones (7), social approval (8).

**The nine learned wants,** which support but never lead: to be informed,
curiosity, cleanliness, efficiency, convenience, dependability, beauty,
economy, bargains.

One rule carries most of the weight. Lead with a single LF8, and when the copy
can only reach a learned want, dig, because an LF8 is sitting underneath it.

## Narrow the set for your own audience

Whitman wrote for consumer direct response, so for many audiences half the LF8
never fire. Decide once which are live for your buyer, write it down, and stop
forcing the rest.

A worked example, for a B2B professional audience on social and email: #2, #4
and #7 rarely fire. The live set is usually:

- **#6 Superiority.** Be the one who is ahead of the room.
- **#8 Social approval.** Be recognized for the work rather than invisible.
- **#3 Freedom from fear.** Stop falling behind while the job changes.
- **#1 Survival**, which shows up as career survival.

The common trap: "learn X" and "here is a useful tool" are the learned want
*to be informed* wearing a costume. Nobody wants to learn the tool. They want
to avoid being left behind, which is #3, or to be the one who gets it, which
is #6. Dig before drafting.

For client work or another business, all eight are in play and your own
narrowing does not apply.

## What survives a sceptical reader, and what collides

The reason to load this skill rather than recall the book. Five moves transfer
cleanly, four read as tactics to a modern audience, and one depends on the
surface.

**Transfers cleanly:**

- **Specificity over superlatives.** "I read 200 posts in 20 minutes" beats
  "this saves me time."
- **The headline carries the load.** The desire promise lives in the first
  line and is never teased for later.
- **One vivid example over a pile of statistics.** Open on the worked case and
  let the numbers back it.
- **Fear needs a specific threat and a doable fix.** Test the threat: if forty
  competitors could run the same fear line ("the old manual way"), it is too
  generic to land.
- **Evidence closes.** Proof for any results claim traces to a real source,
  never to memory and never to a plausible number. When there is no receipt
  for the claim the piece wants to make, change the shape of the piece rather
  than softening the number: report the thing actually done, or pick a
  different proof. Say so in the handoff so the gap is visible.

**Collides, and plain voice wins:**

- **The damaging admission, in its book shape.** Whitman's form is "It's not
  the cheapest. But here is why it outlasts everything cheaper." That
  negation-then-pivot construction has become one of the most recognizable
  tells of templated and AI-written copy, and folding it into "it is durable,
  not cheap" keeps the same tell. Concede in a full sentence that states the
  cost plainly, then move on.
- **Repetition and redundancy.** Principle 15 wants the core message hammered
  across the piece. Repeat the idea, never the construction. Starting sentence
  after sentence the same way reads as a template.
- **Ego morphing and the outcome claim.** Selling the transformed self
  outruns the evidence. The claim is allowed once the receipt is on the page,
  and not before it.

  Split the promise beat before applying this. "In 60 seconds you'll know how
  to X" is a contract about what this piece delivers; it earns retention and
  is settled the moment the piece delivers. Keep it. "Your reply rate will
  never look the same" is a claim about the reader's results, and that one
  waits for the receipt. A single sentence often carries both halves; cut the
  second, keep the first.
- **The restate-the-benefit close.** Whitman closes by repeating the core
  promise next to the CTA. A stronger close is an earned line: the argument
  resolves into one compressed sentence the reader could not have had at the
  start. Restating the benefit spends that slot on something the reader
  already has.

**Surface-dependent:**

- **Rhetorical questions.** Live for short-form video, where the question-hook
  is principle 16 doing real work. Risky in feed posts and email, where a
  reader who knows you reads withholding as a tactic. Name the topic instead
  on those surfaces.

**One hard stop.** Whitman's principle 11, admitting persuasive intent, is
fine at the level of "yes, this is an ad." Do not narrate how your own
audience is being targeted, including which LF8 you are aiming at them.
Demonstrating a technique the piece is teaching is fine. Revealing the
persuasion stack applied to this audience is not.

## The means-end chain

The one piece of mechanics worth keeping in front of you, because drafts skip
it and land on features. Climb feature, then functional benefit, then emotional
payoff, then the LF8. "22% vitamin C" becomes "fewer dark spots" becomes "you
stop avoiding photographs" becomes #8 social approval. A draft that stops at
the functional benefit is the most common failure this skill catches.

## Handoff

Name the chosen LF8 explicitly when handing back, along with the means-end
ladder and any belief triggers used. `copy-strategist` needs the LF8 by number
for step 4.

If you have a voice guide or copy checker, anything drafted or rewritten here,
including suggested alternative lines, goes through it before handing back.

## Boundaries

It cannot verify that a claim is true, and it does not decide where the ethical
line sits on a fear appeal. Both are the human's call. It does not replace
legal review of advertising claims, and it makes no promise about commercial
results.
